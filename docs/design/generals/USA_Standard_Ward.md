## Background

> "No single weapon wins a war. Victory belongs to the commander who knows how to use them all."

General Nathaniel Ward built his reputation not through overwhelming firepower or technological superiority, but through his ability to coordinate every element of the battlefield into a single, devastating fighting force.

A graduate of the United States Military Academy at West Point, Ward began his career as an armored officer. His early deployments exposed him to the limitations of conventional military doctrine, particularly the dangers of relying too heavily on a single branch of the armed forces.

During a disastrous multinational operation, Ward witnessed an entire armored task force become isolated after its air support was redirected and its supply lines severed. Although he successfully organized a fighting withdrawal, the experience fundamentally changed his approach to warfare.

He became a fierce advocate of integrated operations, arguing that infantry, armor, aviation, artillery, intelligence and logistics should function as parts of one coordinated system rather than independent fighting forces.

His nickname, "Atlas", originated during **Operation Iron Horizon**, when Ward assumed command of a collapsing coalition offensive. With communications disrupted and multiple allied formations surrounded, he coordinated a complex counteroffensive involving armored breakthroughs, airborne reinforcements, precision airstrikes and emergency logistics corridors. Against considerable odds, Ward stabilized the front and rescued thousands of coalition personnel. His fellow officers began calling him Atlas, the man who could carry the weight of an entire battlefield on his shoulders.

As global tensions escalated into the conflicts of World Divide, Ward was appointed commander of the United States Joint Expeditionary Command. Unlike generals specializing in airpower, armored warfare or experimental weapons, Ward commands the full spectrum of American conventional military capabilities. He believes America's greatest advantage is not any individual weapons system, but its ability to combine them into a flexible, coordinated fighting force.

## Personality

Ward is disciplined, pragmatic and exceptionally calm under pressure. He avoids unnecessary risks but understands that decisive victories sometimes require bold action.

He respects technological innovation but distrusts commanders who become dependent on it. To Ward, a well-trained infantry platoon working alongside armored support can be as valuable as the most advanced aircraft.

Although respected throughout the armed forces, he occasionally clashes with specialized commanders who believe their preferred weapons should dominate American military strategy. Ward has a simple response:

> "You can win a battle with a tank. You can win a battle with an aircraft. But you win a war with an army."

## In-game identity

- **Type:** Balanced / combined arms.
- **Strength:** coordinated infantry, armor, artillery and air support.
- **Weakness:** no extreme specialisation or exclusive superweapon advantage.

Ward is the standard USA faction experience: the faction's broad conventional arsenal, without the specialised advantages of the other American generals. His identity emphasises adaptability, balanced force composition and tactical coordination, not being a weaker version of the specialists. In line with `DESIGN_FOUNDATION.md` §2 and §5 this comes from the breadth of his roster, not from a combined-arms bonus.
